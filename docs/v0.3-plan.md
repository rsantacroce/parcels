# v0.3 plan: a region of cities, and transport through the ages

Status: proposal. Tracked in the [v0.3.0 milestone](https://github.com/rsantacroce/parcels/milestone/1).
Work happens on `release/v0.3`; features branch from it and merge back, and it
merges to `main` when the release is ready.

## 1. Cities, not parcels

**Today:** one city, cut into parcels. Every player builds inside a rectangle of
a shared grid, and networks run straight across the borders.

**Proposed:** a **region** holding several **cities**. Each player founds and
runs their own city. Between the cities is open country: farmland, forest,
rivers, lakes and coast that nobody owns at the start.

- **City limits.** A city starts as a site the player picks on the region map,
  and its boundary grows with population (or is bought tile by tile), so
  cities can expand toward each other and compete for land, river frontage and
  coast.
- **Connections are built.** Power, water and goods only flow between cities
  along something that physically links them: a highway, power line, pipeline
  or rail line through the countryside. Whoever builds the link pays for it and
  can charge for it.
- **Regional demand** stays shared. People and businesses choose between
  cities by land value, taxes, jobs and travel time. Transport (section 2) is
  how a city wins them.
- **Scoring** stays treasury plus land value, plus a share of regional trade
  and traffic passing through.
- **Unchanged:** one deterministic simulation, commands as the only way to
  change state, lockstep networking, AI and humans as interchangeable
  controllers. `Controller` moves from parcels to cities.

Breaking changes: the save format (parcel to city), the map generator (regions
with sites), the AI (choosing a site, expanding, linking), the UI (a region view
plus a city view) and the networking snapshot.

**Open questions**
1. One big shared grid (the camera can fly from city to city), or a region
   overview whose cities are each their own grid? A shared grid keeps the
   current tech; separate grids allow much bigger regions.
2. Can a city be founded later in the game (a late joiner starts a new town),
   or only at the start?
3. Can cities merge or be annexed?

## 2. Eras: the city evolves over time

Game time moves through **eras**. Each unlocks new buildings and transport,
changes what the people want and lets the look of the city move on.

| Era | Roughly | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Town | years 0–2 | streets, buses, small zones, coal, wind |
| 2. Rail | years 2–4 | rail lines and stations, freight yards, avenues, gas power |
| 3. Metro | years 4–6 | subway lines and stations, dense zones, hospitals, universities |
| 4. Air and sea | years 6+ | airports with flights between cities, ferry terminals and routes, nuclear, landmarks |

Era years live in `config/balance.ron` and are scaled by game length. An option
could unlock eras by population instead of time.

## 3. Transport

All of these need one new piece in the sim: **a commute model**. Today traffic is
a land-value penalty and cars are cosmetic. v0.3 needs a cheap, deterministic
estimate of trips (home to work, shop, school), assigned to the fastest mode
available. Without it, transit can't do anything measurable. A first version
can be aggregate (trips per zone pair over a coarse grid), not per agent.

| Mode | Build | Sim effect | Visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bus** | depot + stops on existing roads; a route is a list of stops | moves trips off cars, cuts traffic, land value near stops | buses following the route, stops with shelters |
| **Train** | rail track (own tiles, bridges, crossings), stations, freight yard | city-to-city passengers and freight; industry near freight gets a boost | trains on track, level crossings, stations |
| **Subway** | underground lines (no surface tiles) + entrances | high capacity inside dense areas; unlocks the densest towers | transit overlay showing lines; entrances on the surface |
| **Airplane** | airport (already 4×4) + runways; routes to other cities and outside the region | regional demand for commerce, offices and tourism; noise | planes taking off, landing and taxiing |
| **Ferry** | terminals on river, lake or coast; routes over water | links cities across water without bridges | ferries crossing |

Each mode has build cost, upkeep, fares (income), capacity and a coverage
radius around stops and stations.

## 4. Order of work

1. Commute model and transit overlay (foundation for everything else).
2. Buses (only roads needed, and they prove the commute model).
3. Eras and unlocks.
4. Region of cities (the large refactor: map, state, saves, AI, UI, net).
5. Trains (most useful once there are several cities).
6. Subway.
7. Airplane routes and ferries.
8. Asset catalog entries for the new vehicles and buildings, website and manual.
